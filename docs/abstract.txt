Replay-strategy comparisons under non-stationarity — a design choice every continually adapting agent embeds — are normally reported as a ranking over policies, plus a claim that the ranking depends on the metric. We evaluate 8 adaptation policies at a fixed update and replay-sample budget across 5 drifting environments, with 10 seeds for each locomotion task and 5 for the process-control case. Selection-aware within-seed permutation tests separate best from worst in 3 of 5 environments after multiplicity correction, but the modal winner keeps rank one in as little as 0.47 of seed resamples. Testing all policy pairs before inspecting selected adjacencies protects the leaderboard analysis; at this seed count the exact-test p-value grid prevents any Holm rejection, so absence of rejection does not establish equal performance. A coarse recency-versus-non-recency contrast survives in 2 of 5 environments under a paired test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Pairing removes a shared seed effect carrying up to 0.91 of return variance. For metric disagreement, we estimate a concordant noise floor separately for each policy pair before averaging. 3 of 24 comparisons receive nominal BH flags, reduced to 2 of 12 when a related improvement metric is removed; these remain exploratory under model-dependent, nonsmooth inference. A Hopper-derived power simulation describes one uncorrected test and supplies no exclusion bound for other environments. Purely recent replay reverses its advantage across environments, and equal update/sample budgets leave wall-clock differing by 1.21–1.45× in this implementation.
